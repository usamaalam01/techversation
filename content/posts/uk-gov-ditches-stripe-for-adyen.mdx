## Introduction to the Change

In a recent development, the UK government has announced that it is replacing Stripe with Adyen as its payment processor. This decision was reported by [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/public-sector/2026/06/04/govuk-goes-dutch-on-payments-as-it-dumps-stripe/5250763), citing the government's desire for more flexibility and customization in its payment systems. As a developer, it's essential to understand the implications of this change and what it might mean for the future of payment processing.

## Why it Matters to Developers

The UK government's decision to switch from Stripe to Adyen is significant for developers because it highlights the importance of customization and flexibility in payment systems. Adyen's platform is known for its ability to be tailored to specific use cases, which may have been a key factor in the government's decision. This trend towards customization could have a ripple effect throughout the industry, with more companies seeking out payment processors that can be adapted to their unique needs.

### Adyen's Technical Advantage

Adyen's technical capabilities may have also played a role in the government's decision. The company's platform is built on a modular architecture, allowing for easy integration with existing systems. Additionally, Adyen's API is designed to be highly customizable, giving developers a high degree of control over the payment process. For example, Adyen's API allows developers to create custom payment flows using a combination of API calls and webhooks:
```python
import requests

# Create a new payment session
response = requests.post('https://live.adyen.com/v1/sessions', json={
    'amount': {
        'currency': 'GBP',
        'value': 1000
    },
    'reference': 'UKGOV-12345',
    'returnUrl': 'https://example.com/return'
})

# Get the payment session ID
session_id = response.json()['id']

# Create a new payment method
response = requests.post(f'https://live.adyen.com/v1/sessions/{session_id}/paymentMethods', json={
    'type': 'card',
    'number': '4111111111111111',
    'expiryMonth': '12',
    'expiryYear': '2025',
    'cvc': '737'
})

# Get the payment method ID
payment_method_id = response.json()['id']
```
This level of customization and control may have been attractive to the UK government, which requires a high degree of flexibility in its payment systems.
