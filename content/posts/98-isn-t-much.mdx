## Introduction

A recent article, [98% Isn't Much](https://whynothugo.nl/journal/2026/07/03/98-isnt-very-much/), has sparked a debate in the software development community about the limitations of AI accuracy. The article argues that even with a high accuracy rate of 98%, AI systems can still make significant mistakes. This has led to a reevaluation of the role of AI in software development and the potential risks associated with relying on these systems.

## The Problem with 98% Accuracy

At first glance, 98% accuracy may seem impressive. However, as the article points out, this means that 2 out of every 100 decisions made by the AI system will be incorrect. In certain applications, such as safety-critical systems or high-stakes financial transactions, this error rate can be catastrophic. The article highlights the importance of considering the consequences of AI mistakes, rather than just focusing on the overall accuracy rate.

### Real-World Implications

The implications of this finding are far-reaching. As AI becomes more integrated into software development, the potential for errors increases. This can lead to a range of problems, from minor bugs to major security vulnerabilities. For example, an AI system that is responsible for generating code may introduce errors that are difficult to detect, leading to downstream problems in the development process.
