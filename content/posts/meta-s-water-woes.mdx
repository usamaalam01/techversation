## Introduction

The recent suspension of Meta's data center water discharges in Cheyenne has sparked a heated debate about the environmental impact of data centers. According to a report by [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/cheyenne-suspends-data-center-fill-and-flush-and-closed-loop-discharges-after-meta-contractor-contaminated-its-reuse-water-system), a contractor contaminated the reuse water system, leading to the suspension. This incident is a wake-up call for the tech industry, highlighting the need for sustainable practices and environmental responsibility.

## The Incident

The incident occurred when a Meta contractor contaminated the reuse water system, which is designed to conserve water and reduce waste. The contamination led to the suspension of water discharges from the data center, which is a significant setback for Meta's operations. The company has since apologized for the incident and is working to rectify the situation. However, the incident raises questions about the adequacy of Meta's environmental protocols and the need for more stringent regulations.

### Sustainable Data Centers

Sustainable data centers are designed to minimize energy consumption and water usage while maximizing efficiency. They use advanced cooling systems, renewable energy sources, and water conservation techniques to reduce their environmental footprint. For example, some data centers use [air-side economization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-side_economizer), which uses outside air to cool the data center, reducing the need for mechanical cooling systems.
