## Introduction

The concept of a "papers, please" era of the internet, where users are required to provide identification and verification to access online services, is becoming a harsh reality. This trend, discussed in a recent post on [Hacker News](https://expression.fire.org/p/the-papers-please-era-of-the-internet), has significant implications for user privacy and security. As the internet becomes increasingly regulated, developers must consider the impact of their work on users' rights.

## Why it Matters to Developers

The "papers, please" era of the internet matters to developers because it affects the way they design and implement online services. With the increasing demand for identification and verification, developers must balance the need for security with the need to protect user privacy. This can be a challenging task, as it requires careful consideration of the type of data collected, how it is stored, and how it is used.

## Broader Implications

The "papers, please" era of the internet has broader implications for society as a whole. As the internet becomes increasingly regulated, there is a risk that users' rights will be eroded. The requirement for identification and verification can be used to suppress free speech, monitor online activity, and discriminate against certain groups of people.

### Community Sentiment

The community sentiment around the "papers, please" era of the internet is one of concern and mistrust. Many users feel that the requirement for identification and verification is an invasion of their privacy and a threat to their online security. Developers, on the other hand, are torn between the need to comply with regulations and the need to protect user rights.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, the "papers, please" era of the internet is a trend that has significant implications for user privacy and security. Developers must consider the impact of their work on users' rights and balance the need for security with the need to protect user privacy. As the internet becomes increasingly regulated, it is essential to have open and honest discussions about the implications of this trend and to work towards finding solutions that protect user rights while also ensuring online security.
