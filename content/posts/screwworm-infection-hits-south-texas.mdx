## Introduction to Screwworm Infection
The recent confirmation of a flesh-eating screwworm infection in South Texas has significant implications for the region's livestock and human health. According to a report by [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/health/2026/06/flesh-eating-screwworm-infection-detected-in-south-texas-usda-says/), this is the first time the parasite has breached the US-Mexico border. The USDA's confirmation of the case has sparked concerns about the potential spread of the disease and the need for effective disease surveillance and tracking systems.

## Impact on Livestock and Human Health
The screwworm infection, caused by the parasite *Cochliomyia hominivorax*, can have devastating effects on livestock, leading to significant economic losses for farmers and ranchers. The parasite's larvae feed on the flesh of infected animals, causing severe tissue damage and potentially leading to death. While the risk of human infection is low, it is not impossible, and cases of human screwworm infestation have been reported in the past. The detection of this parasite in South Texas highlights the importance of monitoring and tracking disease outbreaks to prevent their spread.
