## Introduction to the Stop Killing Games Initiative

The Stop Killing Games initiative has been making waves in the gaming community, with a petition that garnered an impressive 1.3 million signatures. The petition aimed to persuade the European Union to enact laws that would protect games from being destroyed or made unplayable due to technological obsolescence. However, despite the overwhelming support, the initiative has failed to secure EU law. In this post, we'll delve into what happened, why it matters to developers, and the broader implications of this outcome.

## What Happened

The Stop Killing Games initiative was launched in response to the growing concern that many classic games are being lost due to the rapid pace of technological advancements. As new hardware and software emerge, older games become incompatible, and many are left to gather dust. The initiative sought to address this issue by advocating for laws that would require game developers and publishers to ensure their games remain playable, even as technology evolves. The petition gained significant traction, with many gamers and developers throwing their weight behind the cause. However, despite the impressive number of signatures, the EU has declined to enact laws that would protect games from being destroyed.

## Why it Matters to Developers

The failure of the Stop Killing Games initiative has significant implications for game developers. Many developers pour their hearts and souls into creating games, only to see them become unplayable due to technological obsolescence. This not only affects the developers but also the gamers who have invested time and money into these games. The lack of laws protecting games means that developers may be less inclined to create games that are designed to stand the test of time. Instead, they may focus on creating games that are quickly profitable, rather than investing in games that can be enjoyed for years to come.

### The Impact on Game Preservation

The failure of the Stop Killing Games initiative also raises concerns about game preservation. As games become older, they are often lost due to the degradation of physical media or the shutdown of online servers. This means that many classic games are no longer playable, and future generations of gamers may never experience them. The lack of laws protecting games makes it even more challenging to preserve these games, as developers and publishers may not see the value in investing in preservation efforts.
