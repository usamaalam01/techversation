## Introduction

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have advanced rapidly, enabling them to process diverse inputs such as text, images, audio, and video, and generate textual responses. However, the evaluation of these models has not kept pace. Most existing evaluation benchmarks are limited to isolated tasks and reveal little about whether a model integrates information from multiple sources effectively. In this tutorial, we will discuss the limitations of existing benchmarks and provide a step-by-step guide on how to evaluate MLLMs more comprehensively.

## Limitations of Existing Benchmarks

Existing benchmarks for evaluating MLLMs are often limited to specific tasks, such as visual question answering or image captioning. These benchmarks do not assess the model's ability to integrate information from multiple sources, which is a critical aspect of multimodal processing. For example, a model may perform well on a visual question answering task but struggle to generate coherent text when given a combination of image and audio inputs.

To illustrate this point, consider a model that is trained on a dataset of images with corresponding captions. The model may learn to recognize objects in the images and generate captions that describe the objects, but it may not be able to generate coherent text when given a combination of image and audio inputs. This limitation highlights the need for more comprehensive evaluation benchmarks that assess the model's ability to integrate information from multiple sources.

## Evaluating MLLMs: A Step-by-Step Guide

To evaluate MLLMs more comprehensively, we need to assess their ability to integrate information from multiple sources. Here is a step-by-step guide on how to do this:

### Step 1: Prepare a Multimodal Dataset

The first step is to prepare a multimodal dataset that includes a variety of inputs, such as text, images, audio, and video. The dataset should be diverse and representative of the types of inputs the model will encounter in real-world applications.

### Step 2: Define Evaluation Metrics

The next step is to define evaluation metrics that assess the model's ability to integrate information from multiple sources. Some possible metrics include:

* Accuracy: measures the model's ability to generate correct responses to multimodal inputs
* Coherence: measures the model's ability to generate coherent text that integrates information from multiple sources
* Fluency: measures the model's ability to generate fluent text that is free of grammatical errors
