## Introduction

The Los Angeles Police Department (LAPD) has made a significant decision to end its contract with Flock, a surveillance giant that provides license plate reader technology. According to a report by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/lapd-lets-contract-with-surveillance-giant-flock-expire-citing-serious-concerns-over-civil-liberties-and-privacy/), the LAPD cited "serious concerns" over civil liberties and privacy as the reason for ending the contract. This decision has sparked a heated debate around the use of surveillance technology in law enforcement and its impact on individual rights.

## What Happened

Flock's technology allows law enforcement agencies to track and monitor vehicles using license plate readers. The company's system can capture and store images of license plates, along with the location and time of the capture. This data can be used to track individuals and their movements, raising concerns about privacy and civil liberties. The LAPD's decision to end its contract with Flock is a significant blow to the company, as the LAPD is one of its largest government customers.

# Example of a simple license plate reader system
import cv2
import numpy as np

# Load the license plate image
image = cv2.imread('license_plate_image.jpg')

# Pre-process the image
gray = cv2.cvtColor(image, cv2.COLOR_BGR2GRAY)
blur = cv2.medianBlur(gray, 5)

# Detect the license plate
contours, _ = cv2.findContours(blur, cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL, cv2.CHAIN_APPROX_SIMPLE)
for contour in contours:
    area = cv2.contourArea(contour)
    if area > 1000:
        x, y, w, h = cv2.boundingRect(contour)
        cv2.rectangle(image, (x, y), (x+w, y+h), (0, 255, 0), 2)

# Display the output
cv2.imshow('License Plate', image)
cv2.waitKey(0)
cv2.destroyAllWindows()
