## Introduction to the Anti-Data Center Movement

The anti-data center movement, led by climate activist Will Lawrence, is reshaping Michigan politics. As reported by [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/anti-data-center-movement-reshaping-michigan-politics/), Lawrence's shift in focus from climate activism to proposing a data center moratorium has significant implications for the tech industry. This movement is driven by concerns over the environmental impact of data centers, particularly their high energy consumption and e-waste generation.

## Why it Matters to Developers

The anti-data center movement matters to developers because it highlights the growing importance of environmental considerations in technology development. As the demand for digital services continues to grow, the energy consumption and e-waste generation of data centers will become increasingly significant. Developers must consider the environmental impact of their applications and services, from design to deployment. This may involve optimizing code for energy efficiency, using sustainable infrastructure, and designing for recyclability.

### Environmental Impact of Data Centers

Data centers are significant contributors to greenhouse gas emissions, primarily due to their high energy consumption. According to the [Natural Resources Defense Council](https://www.nrdc.org/stories/data-centers-are-getting-hotter), data centers account for approximately 2% of global electricity demand. The production and disposal of IT equipment, such as servers and storage devices, also generate significant amounts of e-waste. As the anti-data center movement gains momentum, developers must prioritize sustainability in their work.
