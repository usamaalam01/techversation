## Introduction

A recent post on the Shopify Engineering blog has been making waves in the developer community, as it details the company's decision to replace Redis with MySQL for inventory reservations. This move has sparked a lively discussion on [Hacker News](https://shopify.engineering/scaling-inventory-reservations), with many developers weighing in on the pros and cons of this approach. In this post, we'll summarize what happened, explain why it matters to developers, and provide our take on the broader implications.

## What Happened

Shopify's engineering team faced a scaling challenge with their inventory reservations system, which was previously powered by Redis. As the company grew, the team encountered issues with Redis's ability to handle the increasing load. After evaluating their options, they decided to replace Redis with MySQL, citing the need for a more robust and scalable solution. The team implemented a custom solution using MySQL, which allowed them to better handle the demands of their inventory reservations system.

## Why It Matters to Developers

The decision to replace Redis with MySQL is significant because it highlights the importance of choosing the right technology for specific use cases. Redis is often used for caching and real-time data processing, but it may not be the best fit for every application. In this case, Shopify's engineering team determined that MySQL was better suited for their inventory reservations system, which requires strong consistency and durability guarantees. This decision demonstrates that developers should carefully evaluate their technology choices and be willing to adapt to changing requirements.

### Technical Considerations

From a technical perspective, the decision to use MySQL instead of Redis is interesting because it involves a trade-off between performance and consistency. Redis is known for its high performance and low latency, but it can be more challenging to ensure data consistency and durability. MySQL, on the other hand, provides strong consistency guarantees but may have higher latency and lower performance. The Shopify engineering team likely chose MySQL because the benefits of strong consistency and durability outweighed the potential performance costs.
