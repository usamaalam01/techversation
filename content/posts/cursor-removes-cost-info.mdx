## Introduction
Cursor, a popular tool for developers, has made a significant change to its usage page and CSV export. The company has removed cost information, citing reasons that have not been explicitly stated. This move has sparked a heated debate among users, with many expressing concerns about transparency and trust. In this post, we'll summarize what happened, explain why it matters to developers, and provide our take on the broader implications.

## What Happened
According to a post on the Cursor forum, the company removed cost information from the usage page and CSV export. This change was made without prior notice, leaving many users surprised and confused. The forum post states that the decision was made to "simplify" the usage page, but many users are not buying this explanation. They argue that cost information is essential for budgeting and planning purposes.

## Why It Matters to Developers
For developers, cost information is crucial for making informed decisions about their projects. Without this information, they may struggle to estimate costs, plan budgets, and optimize their workflows. The removal of cost information from the usage page and CSV export makes it difficult for developers to track their expenses and make data-driven decisions. This lack of transparency can lead to mistrust and frustration among developers, who may feel that Cursor is not prioritizing their needs.
