## Understanding DRAM and Its Vulnerabilities

DRAM is a type of semiconductor memory used in computers and other electronic devices. It stores data in capacitors, which are paired with transistors to form memory cells. The volatile nature of DRAM means that it loses its data when power is turned off. Over the years, various vulnerabilities and techniques have been discovered that can manipulate or exploit DRAM, such as Rowhammer attacks, which involve repeatedly accessing rows of memory to induce bit flips in adjacent rows.

The concept of "spaghettifying" DRAM refers to the idea of manipulating or optimizing DRAM's structure or operation in such a way that it resembles a complex, intertwined structure—much like spaghetti. This could potentially lead to breakthroughs in memory density, speed, or security. However, the term and its implications are still largely speculative and are being explored by researchers and developers.
