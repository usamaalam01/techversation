## Introduction to the Problem
Astronauts on long-duration space missions face a range of health challenges, including vision loss. Prolonged exposure to microgravity can cause fluid shifts in the body, leading to increased pressure on the brain and eyes. This can result in a condition known as space-associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS), which can cause blurred vision, double vision, and even permanent vision loss.

## The Proposed Solution
To address this issue, space agencies are turning to technology used in other fields. A device currently used to help octogenarians perform self-administered eye exams could be part of future space missions to monitor astronauts' ocular health. This device uses artificial intelligence to analyze images of the eye and detect potential problems. By using this technology in space, astronauts can receive regular eye exams and potentially prevent vision loss.
