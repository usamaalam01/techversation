## Introduction to GNSS Interference

The Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) is a network of satellites that provide location information to receivers on the ground. GNSS has become an essential component of modern technology, with applications in navigation, mapping, and timing. However, GNSS signals are vulnerable to interference, which can disrupt the accuracy and reliability of location-based services. Recently, a team of researchers [published a paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03673) on tracing a powerful GNSS interference source over Europe, shedding light on the severity of the issue.

## Why it Matters to Developers

The discovery of a powerful GNSS interference source over Europe has significant implications for developers working with location-based technologies. GNSS interference can have severe consequences, including disruptions to critical infrastructure, such as air traffic control and emergency services, and navigation systems, like those used in autonomous vehicles. Developers must consider the potential risks and consequences of GNSS interference when designing and implementing location-based systems. This includes implementing robust interference mitigation techniques, such as signal processing algorithms and error correction mechanisms, to ensure the accuracy and reliability of location-based services.

import numpy as np

def mitigate_interference(signal):
    # Apply a band-pass filter to remove noise
    filtered_signal = np.convolve(signal, [0.1, 0.8, 0.1], mode='same')
    
    # Apply error correction mechanisms, such as checksums or CRCs
    corrected_signal = np.array([x for x in filtered_signal if x > 0.5])
