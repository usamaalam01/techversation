## Introduction

The terms "open source" and "open community" are often used interchangeably, but they do not necessarily mean the same thing. Open source refers to software that is released under a license that allows users to view, modify, and distribute the code. On the other hand, an open community refers to a group of people who collaborate and contribute to a project in a transparent and inclusive manner. In this post, we will explore the differences between open source and open community, and provide guidance on how to build an open community around an open source project.

## The Difference Between Open Source and Open Community

Open source software is characterized by its license, which determines how the software can be used, modified, and distributed. Some popular open source licenses include the MIT License, the Apache License, and the GNU General Public License. These licenses allow users to use, modify, and distribute the software, but they do not necessarily require the project maintainers to engage with the community or accept contributions from outside developers.

On the other hand, an open community is characterized by its transparency, inclusivity, and responsiveness to feedback. An open community is one where anyone can participate, contribute, and provide feedback, and where the project maintainers are responsive to the needs and concerns of the community. Building an open community around an open source project requires more than just releasing the code under an open source license. It requires a deliberate effort to engage with the community, solicit feedback, and incorporate contributions from outside developers.

## Building an Open Community

So, how can you build an open community around an open source project? Here are some tips:

* **Be transparent**: Provide clear and concise documentation about your project, including its goals, roadmap, and contribution guidelines.
* **Be inclusive**: Encourage participation from anyone who is interested, regardless of their background or level of expertise.
* **Be responsive**: Respond promptly to feedback, questions, and contributions from the community.
* **Use open communication channels**: Use public communication channels such as GitHub issues, mailing lists, or forums to discuss project-related topics.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, open source does not imply open community. Building an open community around an open source project requires a deliberate effort to engage with the community, solicit feedback, and incorporate contributions from outside developers. By following the tips outlined in this post, you can create a community-driven project that is open to contributions from anyone. Remember to be transparent, inclusive, and responsive to feedback, and to use open communication channels to discuss project-related topics. With these principles in mind, you can build a thriving open community around your open source project.
