## Introduction to Age Checks on Android

Google has announced that it will expand age checks on Android devices worldwide by the end of the year. This move is part of Google's efforts to provide safer experiences for users, especially children, and to comply with regulations. In this post, we will summarize what happened, explain why it matters to developers, and give our take on the broader implications.

## What Happened

Google announced the expansion of age checks on Android devices through its [Google Play Age Signals API](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/07/google-play-age-signals-api-safer-experiences.html). The API allows developers to access age-related information about their users, enabling them to provide more tailored and safer experiences. The expansion of age checks is a response to growing concerns about online safety and the need for companies to protect their users, especially children.

## Why It Matters to Developers

The expansion of age checks on Android devices matters to developers for several reasons. Firstly, it provides them with a way to access age-related information about their users, enabling them to provide more tailored and safer experiences. For example, developers can use the age checks to restrict access to certain features or content that may not be suitable for children. Secondly, the expansion of age checks helps developers to comply with regulations, such as the [General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679) and the [Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA)](https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/rules/rulemaking-regulatory-reform-proceedings/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule). By complying with these regulations, developers can avoid fines and reputational damage.

# Example of how developers can use the Google Play Age Signals API
import requests

def get_age_signals(user_id):
    api_url = "https://www.googleapis.com/androidpublisher/v3/applications/{package_name}/ageSignals/{user_id}"
    response = requests.get(api_url)
    if response.status_code == 200:
        return response.json()
    else:
        return None
