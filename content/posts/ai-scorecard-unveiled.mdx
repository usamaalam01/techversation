## Introduction to the AI Scorecard

In a recent announcement, OpenAI's CFO, Sarah Friar, introduced a [practical AI scorecard](https://openai.com/index/a-scorecard-for-the-ai-age) designed to measure the return on investment (ROI) of AI systems. This scorecard is a significant development in the field of artificial intelligence, as it provides a framework for evaluating the effectiveness of AI models. The scorecard assesses AI systems based on four key metrics: useful work, cost per successful task, dependability, and return on compute.

## Why the AI Scorecard Matters to Developers

The introduction of the AI scorecard has significant implications for developers working with AI systems. By providing a standardized framework for evaluating AI models, the scorecard enables developers to compare the performance of different models and make informed decisions about which models to use in their applications. This is particularly important in industries where AI is being used to drive critical decision-making processes, such as healthcare and finance.

```python
# Example of how to calculate the cost per successful task
def calculate_cost_per_task(tasks, cost_per_task):
    successful_tasks = [task for task in tasks if task['success']]
    cost_per_successful_task = cost_per_task / len(successful_tasks)
    return cost_per_successful_task

# Example usage:
tasks = [
    {'success': True, 'task_id': 1},
    {'success': False, 'task_id': 2},
    {'success': True, 'task_id': 3}
]
cost_per_task = 100
cost_per_successful_task = calculate_cost_per_task(tasks, cost_per_task)
print(f"Cost per successful task: {cost_per_successful_task}")
```
