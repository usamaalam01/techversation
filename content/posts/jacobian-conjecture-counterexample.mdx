## What is the Jacobian Conjecture?
The Jacobian conjecture is a mathematical problem that deals with the behavior of polynomial maps. In essence, it states that if a polynomial map has a non-zero Jacobian determinant, then it is invertible. The conjecture has far-reaching implications for various fields, including computer science, algebra, and geometry.

## The Counterexample
The recently discovered counterexample, as explained by [Terry Tao](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/07/21/a-digestion-of-the-jacobian-conjecture-counterexample/), shows that the Jacobian conjecture is false. This means that there exist polynomial maps with non-zero Jacobian determinants that are not invertible. The counterexample is based on a clever construction of a polynomial map that satisfies the conditions of the conjecture but is not invertible.
