## Introduction
The way we approach vulnerability reports is changing. A recent article on [Hacker News](https://words.filippo.io/vuln-reports/) suggests that vulnerability reports are no longer viewed as special, but rather as just another type of bug report. This shift in perception has significant implications for developers and the software industry.

## What's Changing
In the past, vulnerability reports were treated with a high level of urgency and sensitivity. They were often handled by special teams, and the process of reporting and fixing vulnerabilities was typically shrouded in secrecy. However, as the software development process has matured, the way we approach vulnerability reports has changed. With the advent of modern development practices such as continuous integration and continuous deployment (CI/CD), and the increased use of open-source software, the process of reporting and fixing vulnerabilities has become more transparent and streamlined.

## Why it Matters to Developers
The de-emphasis on vulnerability reports has significant implications for developers. On the one hand, it means that developers can focus on fixing vulnerabilities as part of their regular workflow, rather than having to follow a separate and often cumbersome process. On the other hand, it also means that developers need to be more proactive in identifying and reporting vulnerabilities, as the process is no longer handled by a separate team.

```python
    # Create a new issue in the project's issue tracker
    issue = create_issue(vulnerability)
    # Assign the issue to the relevant developer
    assign_issue(issue)
    # Notify the development team of the new issue
    notify_team(issue)
```

## Broader Implications
The shift in perception of vulnerability reports is also reflective of a broader trend in the software industry. As software development becomes more agile and iterative, the distinction between different types of bug reports is becoming less relevant. Instead, developers are focusing on fixing all types of bugs, including vulnerabilities, as part of their regular workflow.


## Conclusion
The de-emphasis on vulnerability reports is a reflection of the increasing maturity of the software development process. As developers, we need to be aware of this shift and adapt our workflows accordingly. By focusing on fixing all types of bugs, including vulnerabilities, as part of our regular workflow, we can create more secure and reliable software.
