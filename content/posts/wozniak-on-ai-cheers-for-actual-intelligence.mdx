## Introduction

In a recent graduation speech, Apple co-founder Steve Wozniak made headlines by telling students that they have something that AI systems lack: actual intelligence. This statement has sparked a heated debate about the role of AI in education and the future of work. As a developer, it's essential to understand the implications of Wozniak's comments and how they relate to the current state of AI research.

## The Context of Wozniak's Comments

Wozniak's speech was not just a sentimental appeal to the graduating class; it was a call to action for developers and educators to rethink the way we approach AI. With the rapid advancement of AI technologies, there is a growing concern that machines will replace human workers, leading to significant job displacement. Wozniak's comments suggest that this fear is misplaced, and that human intelligence is still essential for solving complex problems and driving innovation.

## Why It Matters to Developers

As developers, we are at the forefront of AI research and development. We are responsible for creating the algorithms and models that power AI systems. Wozniak's comments highlight the need for us to focus on creating AI that complements human capabilities, rather than replacing them. This requires a fundamental shift in the way we approach AI development, from a focus on automation to a focus on augmentation.

For example, instead of building AI systems that can perform tasks autonomously, we should focus on building systems that can assist humans in performing tasks more efficiently and effectively. This approach requires a deep understanding of human cognition and behavior, as well as the development of AI systems that can learn from humans and adapt to new situations.

```python
# Example of a human-AI collaboration framework
import numpy as np

class HumanAIModel:
    def __init__(self, human_input, ai_output):
        self.human_input = human_input
        self.ai_output = ai_output

    def collaborate(self):
        # Combine human input with AI output to generate a collaborative output
        collaborative_output = np.concatenate((self.human_input, self.ai_output))
        return collaborative_output

# Initialize the human-AI model
human_input = np.array([1, 2, 3])
ai_output = np.array([4, 5, 6])
model = HumanAIModel(human_input, ai_output)

# Generate a collaborative output
collaborative_output = model.collaborate()
print(collaborative_output)
```

Wozniak's comments have broader implications for the future of work and education. As AI systems become more prevalent, there is a growing need for workers who can collaborate with machines to solve complex problems. This requires a new set of skills, including the ability to communicate with machines, understand their limitations, and adapt to new situations.
