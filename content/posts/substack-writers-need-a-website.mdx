## Introduction

A recent article by Elizabeth Tai, [published on her website](https://elizabethtai.com/2026/06/10/substack-writers-you-need-a-website/), emphasizes the importance of Substack writers having their own website. This advice may seem counterintuitive, given that Substack provides a platform for writers to publish and monetize their content. However, having a personal website can offer numerous benefits, including increased control, flexibility, and branding opportunities.

## Why Substack Writers Need a Website

Substack is a popular platform for writers to share their work and connect with readers. However, relying solely on Substack can limit a writer's ability to build their personal brand and control their online presence. By having a website, writers can:

* Showcase their work in a centralized hub, making it easier for readers to access and discover their content
* Build their personal brand and establish a unique identity
* Reduce dependence on third-party platforms, which can change their algorithms or policies at any time
* Increase flexibility in terms of design, layout, and functionality

## Technical Considerations

Creating a website can seem daunting, especially for writers who are not familiar with web development. However, with the numerous website builders and content management systems available, it's easier than ever to create a professional-looking website. Some popular options include:

* WordPress: A popular content management system with a wide range of themes and plugins
* Ghost: A simple, minimalistic platform designed specifically for bloggers and writers
* Jekyll: A static site generator that allows for customization and flexibility

When choosing a platform, consider factors such as ease of use, customization options, and integration with Substack. For example, you can use Substack's API to fetch your latest posts and display them on your website.

```python
import requests

# Fetch latest posts from Substack API
response = requests.get('https://api.substack.com/posts')
posts = response.json()

# Display posts on your website
for post in posts:
    print(post['title'])
    print(post['content'])
```

## Broader Implications

The advice to Substack writers to create their own website has broader implications for the online publishing industry. As platforms like Substack, Medium, and Twitter continue to evolve, it's essential for writers to maintain control over their online presence. By having a personal website, writers can:

* Build a loyal audience and reduce dependence on algorithm-driven platforms
* Experiment with new formats, such as podcasting or video content
* Monetize their content through advertising, sponsorships, or affiliate marketing
