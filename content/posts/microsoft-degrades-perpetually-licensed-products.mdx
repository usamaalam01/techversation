## Introduction

In a move that has sparked controversy, Microsoft has announced that it will be degrading the functionality of its perpetually-licensed offline products, including Microsoft Office 2019 and 2021 for Mac. According to a report on [Consumer Rights Wiki](https://consumerrights.wiki/w/Microsoft_Office_2019_and_2021_for_Mac_view-only_conversion_(2026)), the company will be converting these products into view-only mode, effectively rendering them useless for editing and creation purposes.

## What Happened

The change affects users who have purchased perpetual licenses for Microsoft Office 2019 and 2021 for Mac. These licenses were meant to provide users with a one-time payment option, allowing them to use the software without any recurring subscription fees. However, with the latest update, Microsoft has decided to limit the functionality of these products, making them incompatible with newer file formats and disabling editing capabilities.

## Why It Matters

This move has significant implications for developers and users who rely on these products. Many businesses and individuals have invested in perpetual licenses, expecting to have full control over their software and data. The sudden degradation of functionality can cause disruptions to workflows, forcing users to either upgrade to subscription-based models or seek alternative solutions.

For developers, this change raises concerns about the longevity and reliability of Microsoft's products. If the company can unilaterally decide to limit the functionality of its software, it undermines the trust and confidence that developers have in the platform. This can lead to a decline in adoption and a shift towards more open-source and community-driven alternatives.

## Broader Implications

The decision to degrade perpetually-licensed products is a clear indication of Microsoft's push towards subscription-based models. The company has been promoting its Microsoft 365 suite, which offers a range of productivity tools and services for a monthly or annual fee. By limiting the functionality of its offline products, Microsoft is attempting to nudge users towards its cloud-based offerings.

This shift towards subscription-based models has both positive and negative consequences. On the one hand, it provides users with access to the latest features and updates, as well as cloud-based storage and collaboration tools. On the other hand, it can lead to vendor lock-in, making it difficult for users to switch to alternative solutions.

## Conclusion

Microsoft's decision to degrade the functionality of its perpetually-licensed offline products is a significant development in the software industry. It highlights the company's push towards subscription-based models and raises concerns about the reliability and longevity of its products. As developers and users, it is essential to be aware of these changes and to consider the implications for our workflows and businesses. Ultimately, the shift towards subscription-based models will require a careful evaluation of the costs and benefits, as well as a consideration of alternative solutions that prioritize user freedom and control.
