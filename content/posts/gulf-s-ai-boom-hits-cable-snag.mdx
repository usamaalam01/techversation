## Introduction to the Problem
The Gulf region is experiencing an AI boom, with numerous businesses and organizations investing heavily in artificial intelligence technologies. However, as reported by [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/the-gulfs-ai-boom-has-an-undersea-cable-problem/), this growth is being hindered by a critical infrastructure issue: undersea cable problems. The region's reliance on these cables for internet connectivity makes them a single point of failure, posing significant risks to AI-dependent businesses.

## Why It Matters to Developers
For developers, the stability and reliability of internet infrastructure are crucial. AI and machine learning applications require vast amounts of data to be transmitted and processed in real-time, making them particularly vulnerable to cable disruptions. A single outage can have far-reaching consequences, including loss of productivity, revenue, and even reputation. As the Gulf region continues to invest in AI, the need for robust and resilient internet infrastructure becomes increasingly important.

## The Impact of Cable Disruptions
Cable disruptions can occur due to various reasons, including natural disasters, human error, or intentional sabotage. The consequences of such disruptions can be severe, with potential losses running into millions of dollars. For example, a study by the [International Cable Protection Committee](https://www.iscpc.org/) estimated that a single cable outage can result in losses of up to $1 million per minute. This highlights the need for hyperscalers and businesses to invest in redundant and diverse infrastructure to mitigate such risks.

## Broader Implications
The Gulf's undersea cable problem has broader implications for the global digital economy. As AI and machine learning continue to transform industries, the need for reliable and resilient internet infrastructure will only increase. The region's experience serves as a warning to other countries and businesses to invest in diverse and redundant infrastructure to mitigate the risks of cable disruptions. Furthermore, it highlights the importance of international cooperation and agreements to protect critical infrastructure and ensure global connectivity.

## Conclusion
The Gulf's AI boom is being hindered by undersea cable problems, posing significant risks to businesses and the regional economy. As the demand for AI and machine learning technologies continues to grow, the need for robust and resilient internet infrastructure becomes increasingly important. Hyperscalers, businesses, and governments must work together to invest in diverse and redundant infrastructure, mitigate risks, and ensure the long-term sustainability of the digital economy. The Gulf region's experience serves as a wake-up call for the global community to prioritize infrastructure development and international cooperation to ensure the continued growth and development of the digital economy.
