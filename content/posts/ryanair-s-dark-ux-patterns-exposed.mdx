## Introduction

A recent post on [Hacker News](https://blog.osull.com/2026/06/12/ryanair-dark-ux-patterns-summer-2026-refresher/) has brought attention to Ryanair's use of dark UX patterns on their website. The low-cost airline has been accused of using deceptive design tactics to trick users into buying additional services, such as travel insurance and seat selection. This controversy has sparked a heated debate among developers and users about the ethics of user experience design.

## What are Dark UX Patterns?

Dark UX patterns refer to design elements that are intended to manipulate or deceive users into taking a specific action. These patterns can include things like hidden fees, misleading language, and cleverly designed interfaces that make it difficult for users to opt-out of certain services. In the case of Ryanair, their website uses a number of these tactics to encourage users to buy additional services.

For example, when booking a flight, users are presented with a series of optional services, such as travel insurance and seat selection. However, the website is designed in such a way that it is easy to accidentally opt-in to these services, and difficult to opt-out. This can result in users being charged for services they do not want or need.

## Why it Matters to Developers

The controversy surrounding Ryanair's use of dark UX patterns is a reminder to developers of the importance of prioritizing user needs and ethics in design. As developers, we have a responsibility to create interfaces that are intuitive, transparent, and respectful of user needs. This means avoiding design patterns that are intended to manipulate or deceive users, and instead focusing on creating interfaces that are clear, concise, and easy to use.

In addition, the use of dark UX patterns can have serious consequences for users, including financial loss and frustration. As developers, we should be aware of these consequences and strive to create interfaces that prioritize user well-being and safety.
