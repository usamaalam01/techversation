## Introduction to Hepatitis C Elimination

England is poised to become one of the first countries to eliminate hepatitis C, a significant medical breakthrough that has far-reaching implications for public health and healthcare systems. According to a report by the BBC, the National Health Service (NHS) has made substantial progress in treating and preventing the spread of the disease, with the number of new cases decreasing dramatically in recent years.

## What Happened

The NHS has been working tirelessly to eliminate hepatitis C, a viral infection that can cause liver damage and liver cancer. The disease is typically spread through blood-to-blood contact, and it is often associated with intravenous drug use. The NHS has implemented a range of measures to combat the disease, including increased screening, improved treatment options, and targeted interventions to reach high-risk groups. As a result, the number of new cases of hepatitis C has decreased significantly, and England is now on track to meet the World Health Organization's (WHO) target of eliminating the disease by 2030.
