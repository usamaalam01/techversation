## Introduction to the Problem
Uber's recent discovery of thousands of items left behind in its robotaxis has sparked interesting discussions about the future of autonomous transportation. According to a [TechCrunch report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/02/squishmallows-dentures-and-an-i-heart-hot-dads-bag-uber-has-found-thousands-of-items-left-in-robotaxis/), the items range from everyday objects like phones and wallets to more unusual items like Squishmallows and dentures. This raises important questions about responsibility and the human factor in autonomous vehicle design.

## Why It Matters to Developers
For developers working on autonomous vehicle projects, this incident highlights the importance of considering the human factor in design. As [robotics and AI technologies](https://www.robotics.org/) continue to advance, it's crucial to think about how passengers will interact with these vehicles and what might be left behind. This could involve designing systems for tracking and returning lost items, as well as implementing safety protocols to prevent accidents or injuries.

### Designing for the Human Factor
When designing autonomous vehicles, developers should consider the following factors:
* How will passengers store and retrieve their belongings?
* What safety features can be implemented to prevent accidents or injuries?
* How will lost items be tracked and returned to their owners?

By considering these factors, developers can create more user-friendly and safe autonomous vehicles. For example, a simple feature like a lost and found system could be implemented using a combination of computer vision and machine learning algorithms. Here's an example of how this could be done in Python:
