## Introduction

The European Union's (EU) efforts to create a unified digital identity system have taken a significant step forward with the introduction of digital ID wallets. However, a recent report has revealed that these wallets are relying on safety services provided by tech giants Google and Apple. This move has sparked intense debate among developers, privacy advocates, and the broader tech community. In this post, we'll delve into the details of this story, explain why it matters to developers, and provide our take on the broader implications.

## What's Happening

According to a report by Waag, a Dutch organization that focuses on the intersection of technology and society, the EU's digital ID wallets are using Google's and Apple's safety services to facilitate secure authentication and verification processes. This means that when EU citizens use their digital ID wallets to access online services, their personal data will be processed and stored on servers operated by these tech giants.

The use of Google's and Apple's safety services is intended to provide an additional layer of security and convenience for users. However, it also raises concerns about data privacy and the potential for these companies to exert undue influence over the EU's digital identity system.

## Why it Matters to Developers

For developers, this story is significant because it highlights the complex trade-offs involved in building secure and user-friendly digital identity systems. On one hand, using established safety services like those provided by Google and Apple can simplify the development process and provide a high level of security. On the other hand, it also means that developers are relying on third-party services that may not be entirely transparent or accountable.

Developers who are building applications that interact with the EU's digital ID wallets will need to consider the implications of this decision. They will need to ensure that their applications are compatible with Google's and Apple's safety services, while also complying with the EU's data protection regulations.

### Security and Privacy Concerns

One of the primary concerns among developers and privacy advocates is the potential for Google and Apple to access and exploit personal data stored on their servers. While both companies have pledged to protect user data, their track records on this issue are not entirely spotless.

For example, Google has faced numerous criticisms over its data collection practices, including the use of location data and browsing history to target advertisements. Apple, on the other hand, has been praised for its commitment to user privacy, but it has also faced scrutiny over its handling of user data in the past.

To mitigate these risks, developers can use secure authentication protocols like OAuth and OpenID Connect, which allow users to authenticate with their digital ID wallets without revealing sensitive information to third-party services. Here's an example of how this can be implemented in Python:
```python
import requests
from oauthlib.oauth2 import WebApplicationClient

# Register your application with the digital ID wallet provider
client_id = "your_client_id"
client_secret = "your_client_secret"
authorization_url = "https://example.com/authorize"

# Redirect the user to the authorization URL
response = requests.get(authorization_url, params={
    "client_id": client_id,
    "redirect_uri": "https://example.com/callback",
    "response_type": "code",
    "scope": "openid profile"
})

# Handle the authorization code and exchange it for an access token
client = WebApplicationClient(client_id)
token_url = "https://example.com/token"
response = requests.post(token_url, headers={
    "Content-Type": "application/x-www-form-urlencoded"
}, data={
    "grant_type": "authorization_code",
    "code": "authorization_code",
    "redirect_uri": "https://example.com/callback",
    "client_id": client_id,
    "client_secret": client_secret
})

# Use the access token to authenticate the user
access_token = response.json()["access_token"]
user_info_url = "https://example.com/userinfo"
response = requests.get(user_info_url, headers={
    "Authorization": f"Bearer {access_token}"
})

# Verify the user's identity and authenticate them
user_info = response.json()
if user_info["sub"] == "expected_user_id":
    # Authenticate the user
    print("User authenticated successfully")
else:
    # Handle authentication failure
    print("Authentication failed")
```
