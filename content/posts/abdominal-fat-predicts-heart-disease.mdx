## The Limitations of BMI

BMI has long been used as a standard measurement for assessing health risks, including heart disease. However, BMI has several limitations. It does not account for muscle mass or body composition, which can lead to inaccurate assessments of health risks. For example, an athlete with a high muscle mass may have a high BMI, but still be at low risk for heart disease. On the other hand, someone with a low BMI but high abdominal fat may be at higher risk for heart disease.

## The Importance of Abdominal Fat Measurements

Abdominal fat, also known as visceral fat, is a type of fat that accumulates around the organs in the abdominal cavity. It has been shown to be a significant predictor of heart disease risk, as it can lead to inflammation and insulin resistance. The new study found that measurements of abdominal fat were more accurate than BMI in predicting heart disease risk. This is because abdominal fat is a more targeted measurement that can provide a more accurate assessment of health risks.
