## Background
Integro-differential equations are a type of mathematical equation that combines integral and differential operators. They are commonly used to model systems with memory, where the current state of the system depends on its past states. In the context of drone stabilization, integro-differential equations can be used to model the dynamics of the drone's angular motion.

The proposed control system consists of a distributed feedback controller that utilizes an integral operator to construct a control signal based on the drone's angular velocity and acceleration. The integral operator has potentially unbounded memory, allowing it to take into account the drone's entire flight history when computing the control signal.

## Distributed Feedback Control
To implement a distributed feedback control law, we need to modify the control signal to depend on the drone's angular velocity and acceleration. One possible approach is to use a proportional-derivative (PD) controller, which can be written as:

$$u(t) = -K_p \theta(t) - K_d \frac{d\theta}{dt}$$

where $K_p$ and $K_d$ are the proportional and derivative gains, respectively.

