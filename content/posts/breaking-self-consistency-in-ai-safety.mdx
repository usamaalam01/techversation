## Introduction to Self-Play and AI Safety

The self-play red team approach is a widely used method for improving AI safety. It involves having different instances of the same model play attacker and defender roles in a zero-sum game. The attacker tries to "jailbreak" the defender, and if self-play converges to a Nash equilibrium, the model is guaranteed to respond safely within the settings of the game. However, this approach has its limitations, particularly when it comes to self-consistency in safety.

## What is Anchored Bipolicy Self-Play?

Anchored bipolicy self-play is a novel approach that breaks self-consistency in safety. It involves training two separate policies: an attacker policy and a defender policy. The attacker policy is trained to maximize the reward, while the defender policy is trained to minimize the reward. The key innovation in anchored bipolicy self-play is the use of an "anchor" policy, which serves as a reference point for the attacker and defender policies.

2.  Initialize the attacker and defender policies.
3.  Train the attacker policy to maximize the reward.
4.  Train the defender policy to minimize the reward.
5.  Update the anchor policy using the attacker and defender policies.
