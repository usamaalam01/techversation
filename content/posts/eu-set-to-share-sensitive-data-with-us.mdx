## Introduction
A recent report by [European Digital Rights (EDRi)](https://edri.org/our-work/the-eu-is-about-to-sell-our-most-sensitive-data-to-the-us-for-visa-free-travel/) has revealed that the European Union is on the verge of sharing sensitive data of its citizens with the United States in exchange for visa-free travel. This move has sent shockwaves across the continent, with many expressing concerns over the potential risks to data protection and security. As a developer, it's essential to understand the implications of this agreement and how it may affect the way we handle sensitive data.

## What's at Stake?
The proposed agreement aims to facilitate visa-free travel between the EU and the US by sharing biometric and other personal data of EU citizens with American authorities. While this may seem like a convenient solution for travelers, it raises significant concerns about data protection and security. The shared data could include sensitive information such as fingerprints, facial recognition data, and other personal details. This has sparked fears among privacy advocates and citizens, who worry that their data could be misused or fall into the wrong hands.

### Best Practices for Data Protection
To mitigate the risks associated with data sharing, developers should follow best practices for data protection. These include:
* Using secure communication protocols, such as HTTPS or SFTP, to transmit sensitive data
* Implementing end-to-end encryption to protect data in transit and at rest
* Limiting access to sensitive data to authorized personnel only
* Regularly monitoring and auditing data access to detect potential security breaches
