## Introduction to Interventional Grounding Audits

Interventional grounding audits are a novel approach to testing the premise dependency of large language models (LLMs). The method involves substituting a target predicate with a fresh symbol and re-running the model to analyze the output. This technique allows developers to determine if the model's chain-of-thought reasoning genuinely depends on its stated premises.

To demonstrate the effectiveness of interventional grounding audits, let's consider an example. Suppose we have an LLM that generates the following chain-of-thought reasoning:

"Pretend you are a detective trying to solve a murder mystery. The victim was found dead in their bedroom. The door was locked from the inside, and there were no signs of forced entry. The only clue is a suspicious letter opener found on the floor."

The LLM might produce a reasoning chain like this:

1. The door was locked from the inside.
2. The victim was found dead in their bedroom.
3. The suspicious letter opener was found on the floor.
4. Therefore, the killer must have been someone who was already in the house.

However, if we substitute the target predicate "locked from the inside" with a fresh symbol, say "X", and re-run the model, we might get a different output. For instance:

1. The door was X.
2. The victim was found dead in their bedroom.
3. The suspicious letter opener was found on the floor.
4. Therefore, the killer must have been someone who was already in the house.

If the model's output remains the same, it suggests that the premise "the door was locked from the inside" is not crucial to the reasoning chain. On the other hand, if the output changes significantly, it indicates that the premise is essential to the model's chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Implementing Interventional Grounding Audits

To implement interventional grounding audits for your LLM, you'll need to follow these steps:

### Step 1: Identify the Target Predicate

Identify the target predicate you want to substitute with a fresh symbol. In our example, the target predicate is "locked from the inside".

### Step 2: Substitute the Target Predicate

Substitute the target predicate with a fresh symbol. You can use a random word or a symbol that is not present in the original text.

### Step 3: Re-run the Model

Re-run the LLM with the modified input and analyze the output.

### Step 4: Compare the Outputs

Compare the output of the original model with the output of the modified model. If the outputs are similar, it suggests that the premise is not crucial to the reasoning chain. If the outputs differ significantly, it indicates that the premise is essential to the model's chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Conclusion

Interventional grounding audits are a valuable tool for testing the premise dependency of large language models. By substituting a target predicate with a fresh symbol and re-running the model, developers can determine if the model's chain-of-thought reasoning genuinely depends on its stated premises. This technique can help improve the transparency and accountability of LLMs, making them more reliable and trustworthy.
