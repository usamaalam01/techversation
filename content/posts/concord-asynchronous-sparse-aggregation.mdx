## Introduction

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has emerged as a pivotal technique for improving language models by incorporating external knowledge at inference time. As device-cloud collaborative inference makes it feasible to deploy small language models on edge devices, a new setting arises in which private documents remain on the device and public knowledge is retrieved from the cloud. CONCORD, a novel approach, enables this setting by using asynchronous sparse aggregation for device-cloud RAG under document isolation.

## Background

Language models have become increasingly powerful in recent years, but they often rely on large amounts of training data to generate high-quality text. However, in many cases, the data is private and cannot be shared with the cloud. This is where retrieval-augmented generation comes in – it allows language models to retrieve external knowledge from the cloud while keeping private documents on the device.

## CONCORD Overview

CONCORD is a technique that enables device-cloud collaborative inference for language models. It uses asynchronous sparse aggregation to retrieve external knowledge from the cloud and aggregate it with the private documents on the device. This approach allows for improved language models while maintaining the privacy of the documents.

### Key Components

* **Asynchronous Sparse Aggregation**: This is the core component of CONCORD. It allows for the aggregation of external knowledge from the cloud with the private documents on the device in an asynchronous manner.
* **Device-Cloud Collaborative Inference**: This component enables the deployment of small language models on edge devices, which can then retrieve external knowledge from the cloud.
