## Introduction to Last.fm's Independence

Last.fm, a popular music streaming platform, has recently announced its independence from CBS, its parent company since 2007. This move is significant, as it allows Last.fm to operate as a standalone entity, making its own decisions about technology, development, and strategy. In this post, we'll explore what this means for developers, the music industry, and the broader implications of this change.

## What Happened

According to the [official announcement](https://support.last.fm/t/last-fm-is-now-independent/118591) on the Last.fm support forum, the company has completed its transition to independence. This means that Last.fm is now free to make its own decisions about its operations, technology, and development, without needing to consult with or seek approval from CBS. This change is expected to bring about significant improvements to the platform, as Last.fm will have more control over its own destiny.

## Why it Matters to Developers

For developers, Last.fm's independence is a significant development. As an independent company, Last.fm will have more freedom to innovate and experiment with new technologies, which could lead to new opportunities for developers to contribute to the platform. Additionally, Last.fm's independence may lead to a more open and collaborative approach to development, with the company potentially releasing more of its code as open-source or partnering with other companies to develop new features.

## Broader Implications

The broader implications of Last.fm's independence are significant. As a standalone company, Last.fm will be able to compete more effectively with other music streaming platforms, such as Spotify and Apple Music. This increased competition could lead to innovation and improvements in the music streaming industry as a whole, as companies strive to outdo each other in terms of features, pricing, and user experience.
