## Introduction

As AI systems become increasingly complex and pervasive, the need for interpretability has grown exponentially. Radical AI interpretability, as introduced in the arXiv paper [2606.26523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26523), offers a novel framework for understanding AI systems as agents. This approach draws on philosophical traditions and mechanistic interpretability tools to uncover the beliefs, desires, and meanings of AI systems. In this tutorial, we will delve into the practical applications of radical AI interpretability, providing step-by-step guidance and working code examples.

## Understanding Radical Interpretability

Radical interpretability is rooted in the philosophical tradition of radical interpretation, which seeks to understand the meaning and content of mental states, such as beliefs and desires. In the context of AI systems, this means analyzing the computational facts about a system to infer its underlying beliefs, desires, and meanings. This approach is essential for ensuring safety in AI applications, as it allows developers to identify potential biases, flaws, or unintended consequences.

## Mechanistic Interpretability Tools

Mechanistic interpretability tools are used to analyze the internal workings of AI systems and uncover their computational facts. These tools can include techniques such as:

* **Model explainability**: Methods for explaining the decisions made by AI models, such as feature importance or partial dependence plots.
* **Model transparency**: Techniques for understanding the internal workings of AI models, such as visualizing neural network architectures or analyzing model weights.

### Example: Model Explainability with SHAP

One popular tool for model explainability is SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations). SHAP is a technique for assigning a value to each feature for a specific prediction, indicating its contribution to the outcome.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestClassifier
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
import shap

# Load dataset
df = pd.read_csv('data.csv')

# Split data into training and testing sets
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(df.drop('target', axis=1), df['target'], test_size=0.2, random_state=42)

# Train random forest classifier
rf = RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=100, random_state=42)
rf.fit(X_train, y_train)

# Create SHAP explainer
explainer = shap.TreeExplainer(rf)

# Get SHAP values for test data
shap_values = explainer.shap_values(X_test)

# Plot SHAP values for a specific instance
shap.force_plot(explainer.expected_value, shap_values[0,:], X_test.iloc[0,:], matplotlib=True)
```

## Implementing Radical Interpretability

To implement radical interpretability, developers can follow these steps:

1. **Define the AI system's goals and objectives**: Clearly articulate the purpose and desired outcomes of the AI system.
2. **Analyze the computational facts**: Use mechanistic interpretability tools to analyze the internal workings of the AI system and uncover its computational facts.
3. **Infer beliefs, desires, and meanings**: Use the computational facts to infer the AI system's beliefs, desires, and meanings.
4. **Evaluate and refine**: Evaluate the results and refine the AI system as needed to ensure safety and alignment with its goals and objectives.
