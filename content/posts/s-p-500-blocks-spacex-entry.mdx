## Introduction

In a significant development, the S&P 500 has rejected SpaceX's entry into the index, citing a rule that requires companies to be profitable. This decision not only affects SpaceX but also has implications for other innovative companies, including OpenAI and Anthropic, which are also not yet profitable. As reported by [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/06/sp-500-blocks-fast-spacex-entry-wont-waive-rule-for-unprofitable-ai-firms/), this move highlights the challenges faced by companies that are driving technological advancements but may not meet traditional financial criteria.

## What Happened

The S&P 500, a stock market index that represents the market value of 500 large, publicly traded companies in the US, has a rule that requires companies to be profitable to be included in the index. SpaceX, which has been expanding its operations and has significant valuation, does not meet this criterion. Despite its growth and potential, the company's financials do not align with the S&P 500's requirements. As a result, SpaceX's entry into the index has been blocked. This decision also affects OpenAI and Anthropic, two prominent AI firms that are not yet profitable.
